The hut

The fishing hut came from an art pack: flat, pinkish-red boards one tone each, its own outline. It's redrawn here from materials, the way the far bank was, and lit from the upper left. It's the same size and layout, so the angler and the barrel still stand on the walkway, the pilings carry on down into the lake, and the windows light up after dark. Only its left side is on screen.

In every version

A working fishing shack

  1. On the hut: a stovepipe with its rain cap, a four-pane window catching the sky, a half-glazed door, a storm lantern on a bracket, a life ring, a coil of rope on a peg.
  2. Below it: squared pilings, lit on one face, with cross braces between them.
  3. On the walkway: plank ends, a railing with a net drying over it, and a new barrel with iron hoops.
  4. After dark: the window, the door's glass and the lantern glow warm.
The three versions

What it's built of

A · Weathered cedar shack

Silver-grey board-and-batten, streaked by weather; a corrugated tin roof rusting where the rain runs off; a grey-blue door.

  • Quiet, sits back into the rock behind it
  • The least colourful

B · Red Northwoods boathouse

Barn-red clapboard with white trim, a green shingle roof, a green door, a flower box of geraniums under the window.

  • Warm and cozy; the classic lake cabin
  • Red beside his red flannel

C · Log cabin

Round logs with light chinking and their ends showing at the corner, a cedar shake roof, natural wood trim and a plank door.

  • Rustic, of a piece with the forest
  • Busy at this size
