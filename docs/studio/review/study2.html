The far bank

Three versions of everything between the hills and the water: the trees, the rock, the shore where the animals come down, and the waterfall. Each one is shown in the game itself, under the time-of-day light, with Olive, the hut and the wildlife in front. Flip between them and the bank you have now.

The three versions

What each one tries

A · Granite point

The Canadian Shield: a pink granite outcrop with spruce, fir and a silver snag along its top, the falls pouring over its flank in tea-coloured water. A big white pine stands over the shore, with spruce and a birch clump, and smooth granite and a lone boulder at the water.

  • The most "up north" of the three
  • The white pine is drawn big this time, so its flat tiers read

B · The bank we have, redrawn

The same layout as now (grassy slope, boulder, two big spruces, a cliff with a tall white waterfall, a treeline on top), drawn in our own materials and light.

  • The least change: everything stays where you know it
  • Grey rock and white water, like today

C · Birch cove

A low bank and a pebble beach where the animals come down, white birches and aspen bright against the dark spruce, a pair of red pines, and a pink granite bluff with the falls a narrow ribbon of tea-coloured water.

  • The brightest and most open at the water
  • The birches turn gold when seasons come
What stays the same in all three

The rules at work

Every piece is drawn at true size on the one pixel grid, from the materials library's ramps. Light comes from the upper left, and each shape is lit as one mass: a rim of light on its top and left, shade on its bottom and right, one calm tone between. Spruce are a few big drooping tiers, each hanging over the one below. Rock is broken into big planes and ledges, not speckled. The forest behind is row under row of treetops, with a hazy band of spires on the far ridge.

The shore along the left stays flat at the water, so the deer, fox, hare and bear still walk down to drink, and the waterfall still runs and throws up its mist.

Honest notes

What isn't right yet

  1. The tea-coloured falls (A and C) are true to north-woods rivers, but at this size they can read a little like a pale log. B's white water reads as water at a glance.
  2. The old bank has richer, warmer greens and big soft clumps on its trees. Ours is darker and more like a real conifer forest; tell me if you'd like it sunnier.
  3. The forest behind is quieter than I'd like, with a hazy band between the far ridge and the near trees.
