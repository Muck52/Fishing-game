Fishing mechanics

Fishing games break a catch into the same few stages. At each stage, what the best-known games do, what players say about it on Steam, the game forums and in reviews, and how well it suits our game: a phone held in one hand, portrait, a lake seen side-on from the surface to the bed. What we already do is marked.

Where and what to fish for, before any line goes in.

1 · Finding the fish

Shadows and signs

Fish are visible as shadows, ripples or birds diving; their size hints at the species before you cast.

Seen in: Animal Crossing (eight shadow sizes), Stardew (bubbling spots), Cast n Chill (the dog alerts you to a good spot)

Players like: Reading the water feels like real angling knowledge; big shadows create excitement.

Players dislike: Little, as long as the hints are honest.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Strong fit: our lake is side-on, so shadows and silhouettes can be seen under the surface. Olive could bark at a good spot.

Conditions decide the catch

Each species keeps its own time of day, weather, season, depth and spot.

Seen in: Stardew, Pokémon, Persona, Cast n Chill (16 spots with their own fish)

Players like: Gives a reason to come back at dawn or in the rain; turns the journal into a puzzle.

Players dislike: Lookup-table grinding when the conditions are hidden or too narrow.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): We already run on the phone's real clock, so the time-of-day half is nearly free.

Bait and lure choice

What's on the hook filters which fish bite: worms for panfish, minnows for walleye, lures for pike.

Seen in: Fishing Life (catches used as bait), Zelda (sinking lure), Entropia (lures as hard filters)

Players like: A small, meaningful decision before every cast.

Players dislike: Inventory fiddling on a small screen.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Good fit if it's one tap: a bait tin of three or four choices.

Getting the line where you want it.

2 · The cast

Tap to cast

One tap; the line goes to a set distance.

Seen in: Webfishing, many idle games

Players like: Instant, zero friction.

Players dislike: No skill or choice at all.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Fine as a fallback; too thin alone.

Hold for power

Hold to charge, release to cast; longer holds cast further.

Seen in: Fishing Life, Stardew (power meter)

Players like: Simple, tactile, lets you aim for deeper water.

Players dislike: Overshooting a spot with no way to correct.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Natural on a phone.

Swipe to cast in our game now

Flick upward; the length and speed of the swipe set the distance.

Seen in: Mobile sports-fishing games

Players like: Feels like a real cast.

Players dislike: Inconsistent on small screens.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): This is what we do now.

Brake and feather

Tap again while the line flies to stop it on the spot.

Seen in: Zelda: Ocarina of Time (brake while casting)

Players like: Precision: drop right on a shadow.

Players dislike: One more thing to learn.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): A nice second layer on top of the swipe.

What you do between the cast and the bite.

3 · Waiting and working the bait

Just wait

Watch the float; the fish comes in its own time.

Seen in: Animal Crossing, Stardew, Webfishing

Players like: Calm, cozy, lets the scene breathe.

Players dislike: Coral Island and Webfishing players call the wait dull when it runs long.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Keep waits short, and keep the scene alive with wildlife (we have a lot).

Species that come to the bait differently in our game now

Each fish approaches, nibbles and takes the bait its own way; the float tells you who it is.

Seen in: Animal Crossing (nibbles, then the plunge), Zelda

Players like: Expert players learn to read a fish before they see it.

Players dislike: Fake nibbles that punish you without a clear tell.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): We already model 17 species' approaches and nibbles; making their tells clearer is cheap.

Jigging and retrieving

Twitch or reel the bait to make it move; some fish only bite a moving lure.

Seen in: Zelda (wiggle the lure), Fishing Life (tap to reel in a little)

Players like: Active, skillful, real angling.

Players dislike: Busywork if it doesn't change which fish come.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): A tap to jig fits; we already animate the float jig.

Depth control

Choose or let the bait sink to the depth where a species lives.

Seen in: Fishing Life (line sinks as you watch), Pokémon (deeper water, rarer fish)

Players like: Our side view shows the whole water column, so depth is visible.

Players dislike: Hard to judge without a gauge.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Great fit: our lake is seen in section, top to bottom.

The moment of truth.

4 · The bite and the hook

Strike on the plunge in our game now

React when the float goes under; too early scares the fish, too late it spits the hook.

Seen in: Animal Crossing (press as the bobber sinks), Stardew (the '!' prompt)

Players like: A sharp, satisfying moment; rarer fish give a shorter window.

Players dislike: Missed bites feel unfair without a clear audio or haptic cue.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): We do this, with a vibration. Players recommend listening for the splash.

Shake or confirm prompts

Tap a prompt (or several) before the fight starts.

Seen in: Fisch (shake icons that move around; miss one and you recast)

Players like: Keeps you engaged while it 'bites'.

Players dislike: Losing the whole cast to one missed prompt.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Weak fit; extra taps without much meaning.

Where most fishing games differ, and where players are most opinionated.

5 · The fight

Keep the fish in the zone in our game now

A fish icon moves up and down a bar; you hold to raise your green zone and keep it on the fish; a progress meter fills while you do.

Seen in: Stardew Valley, Fisch (horizontal), Sun Haven

Players like: Real skill and a clear learning curve; each species moves its own way (carp lazy, lava eel wild).

Players dislike: Hard for reaction speed or hand pain; a regular stream of accessibility requests on the Stardew forum; feels like a minigame, not fishing.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): This is our default fight now, styled as a bar.

Tension: reel, then ease off

Hold to reel in; tension rises; let go when the fish runs or the line snaps; the fish tires.

Seen in: Ace Fishing, Reel Fishing, Cast n Chill, Fishing Clash (keep the tension centred)

Players like: Feels like real fishing: feel the fish, give it line, take it back.

Players dislike: Opaque when the meter isn't readable; deaths by sudden spikes.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Very good on a phone (one thumb); we have this mode in code (hold-reel).

Risk zone bonus

Pushing tension into the red, without snapping, builds a bonus meter for a bigger reward.

Seen in: Ace Fishing (Strength meter)

Players like: A thrilling risk-reward layer on top of tension.

Players dislike: Punishing if the snap is unpredictable.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): A good optional depth layer on top of the tension fight.

Pull against the run

The fish swims left or right; you pull the rod the opposite way; reel only when it tires.

Seen in: Sea of Thieves (rod creaks when you're wrong), Final Fantasy XV (tilt with the fish)

Players like: Reads the fish's body language; rumble and creaks give feedback.

Players dislike: Twitchy on touch screens without a stick.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Possible with left and right swipes; our fish already have 'cover' they run for.

Fish stamina and line durability

Two bars: the fish tires as you hold on, your line wears as it fights; better rods resist.

Seen in: Final Fantasy XV

Players like: Big fish feel like an endurance fight; gear upgrades matter.

Players dislike: Long fights get repetitive.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Pairs well with tension; makes the muskie and sturgeon epic.

Beats and jumps

The fish pulls in rhythmic tugs; tap in time; when it leaps, stop reeling ('bow to the fish').

Seen in: Final Fantasy XV (jump prompts), Zelda Switch (don't reel while it faces away)

Players like: Reacting to a jump is a great moment.

Players dislike: Pure QTEs feel like button prompts, not fishing.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): We have this mode in code (rhythm), with jumps and cover.

Timing rings

Hit a moving marker as it passes targets on a spinning ring; bigger fish give more rings.

Seen in: Dredge

Players like: Fast, readable, works on any input.

Players dislike: One slip loses all progress; no clearly defined hit zone.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Fits touch well, but has nothing to do with the fish's behaviour.

Hold and click

Hold to reel; tap at 'yank' moments; very forgiving.

Seen in: Webfishing

Players like: Never frustrating; social and relaxed.

Players dislike: Little challenge; some reviews call it simplistic.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): A good gentle or accessible mode.

Auto or idle

The fish reels itself in; playing the minigame just speeds it up.

Seen in: Dredge (automatic fallback), Cast n Chill (idle mode, even offline)

Players like: Removes the failure stress; great for relaxing.

Players dislike: Some feel it hollows out the game.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): An easy accessibility or assist option.

The payoff.

6 · Landing it

The catch moment in our game now

The fish is lifted, flops, is weighed and measured; a popup shows the species, size and record.

Seen in: Stardew, Animal Crossing (the pun line), Zelda (weigh-in at the pond)

Players like: The reward beat; records make each catch matter.

Players dislike: Popups that interrupt too often.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): We have the flop, the popup and the journal.

Net it at the dock

A last timing tap to scoop the fish as it comes to the surface.

Seen in: Real angling; some sports-fishing sims

Players like: A tense final moment for big fish.

Players dislike: One more way to lose a fish at the end.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Could be just for big fish.

Arcade payoffs

Fish fly out of the water and you shoot or catch them for points.

Seen in: Ridiculous Fishing (descend dodging, ascend collecting, then shoot)

Players like: Wild, spectacular, very mobile.

Players dislike: Not cozy, and not realistic.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Off-tone for us, but its three-phase descent (dodge on the way down, catch on the way up) is a clever use of a vertical lake like ours.

Long-term systems.

7 · Why you come back

The collection

A journal of every species, with the biggest caught, where and when.

Seen in: Animal Crossing (museum), Stardew, Cast n Chill (record book)

Players like: The single most loved loop; completionists stay for months.

Players dislike: Missing fish with no hints.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): We have the journal; adding conditions as hints would help.

Gear upgrades

Better rods, lines, reels and floats make harder fish catchable.

Seen in: Cast n Chill, Ace Fishing, Fishing Life

Players like: Visible progress; old enemies become easy.

Players dislike: Gear gates and paywalls (Ace Fishing reviewers felt progress slowed).

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Good, kept gentle and earned in-game.

Trophy sizes and records

Each fish has a weight; personal bests and trophy weights.

Seen in: Zelda (weight prizes), Fishing Clash

Players like: Every catch might be a record.

Players dislike: Pure randomness without skill.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Cheap to add on top of the journal.

Bounties and quests

Requests for a certain fish at a certain time, for rewards.

Seen in: Stardew (bundles), Dredge (orders)

Players like: Direction when you don't know what to do next.

Players dislike: Chore lists.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Could come from the field notes and Olive's story.

Idle and offline catches

The game fishes for you while you're away.

Seen in: Cast n Chill, Hooked Inc

Players like: Coming back to a full bucket.

Players dislike: Can make active play feel pointless.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): Optional; it fits a phone game well.

Companion

A pet or friend who helps: alerts, fetches, keeps you company.

Seen in: Cast n Chill (dog points out spots)

Players like: Warmth and charm.

Players dislike: None reported.

For us (phone, one thumb, portrait): We have Olive; she could help as well as keep him company.

What players keep saying

Patterns across the forums

  1. It should feel like fishing, not a button prompt. Tension, giving line and reading the fish win; rhythm prompts and QTEs with no link to the fish lose.
  2. Skill should be learnable, and fail gently. Stardew is praised because you can "git gud", and criticised because some players never can. Dredge's "one slip and it's gone" draws complaints.
  3. Each species should fight its own way. It's what makes a rare catch memorable.
  4. Waiting is fine if the world is alive, and dull if it isn't, or runs long.
  5. Offer an easier path. Accessibility requests (hand pain, reaction speed) are constant for the Stardew fight; Dredge and Cast n Chill let the fish come in on its own.
  6. The collection is the long game. The journal, records and conditions bring players back more than any single fight.
