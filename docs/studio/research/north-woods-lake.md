# The real north-woods lake

Reference for an Upper Midwest (Minnesota / Wisconsin / Michigan) lake at
about 46–48°N, gathered 2026-10-22. Facts come from the agency, university
and field-guide sources linked in each section. **Hex colours are estimates
from typical photos**, not published values. "(general knowledge)" marks
what a search didn't confirm.

## 1. Trees and plants

### Eastern white pine, the skyline tree
- **Shape:** young trees are a neat pyramid. Old ones are flat-topped and
  windswept, rising above the rest of the forest. Their branches grow in
  horizontal tiers, one tier a year, with long bare gaps between flat plates
  of needles, and wind often swings them to one side like a flag.
- **Size:** 80–100 ft, 1.5–2 times taller than the birch and aspen around them.
- **Colour:** soft blue-grey-green, feathery rather than spiky (mid `#4F6B5A`, shadow `#2E4238`, light `#7A9584`).
- **Bark:** grey-brown with a purple tinge, in long furrowed plates (`#5A4F4A`, furrows `#2E2826`).
- Long hanging cones, 4–8 in.
- Sources: [MN DNR](https://dnr.state.mn.us/trees/eastern-white-pine.html), [Minnesota Seasons](https://www.minnesotaseasons.com/Plants/eastern_white_pine.html), [UMN](https://trees.umn.edu/eastern-white-pine-pinus-strobus)

### Red pine (Norway pine), Minnesota's state tree
- **Shape:** a long, straight, bare trunk with a small rounded dome of needles
  only at the top. Plantations stand in rows.
- **Size:** 66–115 ft.
- **Bark is the key colour:** grey to reddish-brown plates low down (`#7A4E3A`),
  flaky bright orange-red higher up (`#C2643A`). Trunks glow orange in evening sun.
- **Needles:** dark yellow-green, in stiff brush-like tufts (`#3F5A2A`).
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_resinosa), [MSU](https://s1.lite.msu.edu/res/msu/botonl/b_online/earle/pi/pin/resinosa.htm)

### Balsam fir, white spruce, black spruce
- **Balsam fir:** a sharp, symmetrical spire, the most Christmas-tree of the three.
  Flat, dark, slightly glossy needles (`#2C4A33`). Smooth grey bark with resin
  blisters. 40–60 ft.
- **White spruce:** a broader pyramid, dark green to bluish (`#3A5548`), cones
  hanging at the tips. 60–80 ft.
- **Black spruce:** a small, very narrow, ragged tree of the bogs, often 15–30 ft,
  with a club of cones at the top (general knowledge). Draw it as thin, shaggy
  bottle-brush spikes along a boggy shore (`#2F4038`).
- Sources: [Minnesota Wildflowers](https://www.minnesotawildflowers.info/tree/white-spruce), [bplant](https://bplant.org/photo/930)

### Paper birch
- **Bark:** chalk-white (`#EDEAE2`) with short black horizontal marks
  (`#2A2626`), peeling in papery curls that are pink-orange underneath
  (`#D9A88A`). Old trunks go black and rough at the base.
- **Form:** often 2–5 stems from one base. An open, airy oval crown, 50–70 ft.
- **Leaves:** mid-green (`#6E8F3E`), bright yellow (`#E6C23A`) from late
  September to early October.
- Source: [Prairie Public](https://news.prairiepublic.org/podcast/natural-north-dakota/2023-01-21/identifying-quaking-aspen-and-paper-birch)

### Quaking aspen ("popple")
- **Bark:** smooth, creamy, pale green-white (`#D6D8C4`), with black eye-shaped
  scars. It doesn't peel.
- **Form:** one stem, a narrow rounded crown. Grows in stands of even height.
- **Leaves:** round, on flat stalks, so they **flutter in any breeze**. In
  pixels that's a random one-pixel flicker of light and dark in the canopy.
  Summer `#7A9A45` with silvery undersides; fall golden `#F0C419`, peaking
  about September 28 to October 8.
- **Fall around it:** maples peak first (mid to late September, red and
  orange). Tamarack, the one conifer that changes, goes golden-orange
  (`#D9A030`) in mid-to-late October. The classic look is gold birch and
  aspen against dark green conifers.
- Sources: [Three Rivers Parks](https://www.threeriversparks.org/blog/species-spotlight-quaking-aspens), [North Shore Explorer](https://northshoreexplorermn.com/guide-to-north-shore-peak-fall-colors/), [MN DNR](https://www.dnr.state.mn.us/state_forests/newsletter-september-2026.html)

### Northern white cedar
- Grows on swampy and rocky shores; trunks often **lean out over the water**,
  roots gripping the rock.
- **Bark:** stringy vertical strips, red-brown when fresh (`#8A5A3C`),
  weathering grey (`#8C8478`), often mossy.
- **Foliage:** flat, fan-like yellow-green sprays (`#5E7A3A`), a narrow
  irregular cone, 40–50 ft. Deer browse it flat underneath about 6 ft up.
- Sources: [Northern Woodlands](https://northernwoodlands.org/outside_story/article/ways-of-cedar), [USFS](https://research.fs.usda.gov/silvics/northern-white-cedar), [Wild Adirondacks](https://wildadirondacks.org/trees-of-the-adirondacks-northern-white-cedar-thuja-occidentalis.html)

### Shoreline plants
- **Cattail:** flat sword-like blades 4–8 ft (`#6B8040`, tan `#B59A62` in fall),
  brown "hot-dog" heads (`#5A3A22`) that go white and fluffy in late fall.
  Mucky bays, not rocky shores.
- **Hardstem bulrush:** round, leafless, dark green stems (`#3E5A34`) 3–8 ft,
  in dense stands in 1–5 ft of water.
- **Wild rice (manoomin):** ribbon-like floating leaves in late May and June,
  then upright plants 3–9 ft. Late-summer beds look like a light green-gold
  lawn on the water (`#A8B25A` to `#C9B270`). Shallow, soft-bottomed bays.
- **White water lily:** round pads 4–12 in with a slit (`#4E7A3A`, purple-red
  under); white many-petalled flowers (`#F8F6F0`) with yellow centres that
  open in the morning and **close in the afternoon** (general knowledge).
- **Spatterdock (yellow pond lily):** bigger notched pads, often standing up
  out of the water; cup-shaped bright yellow flowers (`#E8C21E`).
- **Pickerelweed:** glossy heart-shaped leaves, violet-blue flower spikes in
  July–August, in a band just outside the cattails.
- Sources: [Minnesota Wildflowers](https://www.minnesotawildflowers.info/grass-sedge-rush/wild-rice), [1854 Treaty Authority](https://www.1854treatyauthority.org/wild-rice/biology-of-wild-rice.html), [WA Ecology](https://fortress.wa.gov/ecy/gisresources/lakes/AquaticPlantGuide/descriptions/nuppol.html), [PA DCNR](https://naturalheritage.dcnr.pa.gov/Community.aspx?=15996), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontederia_cordata)

### Underwater plants (all in MN DNR lake surveys)
- **Coontail:** bushy bottlebrush whorls, dark olive (`#3D5228`), in dense
  mounds 5–15 ft down; no roots.
- **Pondweeds:** wavy translucent leaves on tall stems reaching the surface,
  small brownish floating pads, or thin grassy tufts.
- **Wild celery:** long translucent ribbons from a rosette on the bottom,
  swaying with the current, trailing on the surface in late summer
  (`#6E8C3A`, tips `#9AB060`).
- **Chara (muskgrass):** a low grey-green carpet (`#7E8C6A`) on sand in clear
  water: a fuzzy lawn on the bottom.
- **Depth limit:** plants grow only where about 1% of sunlight reaches the
  bottom, roughly 15–20 ft in a typical north-woods lake. Below that the
  bottom is bare.
- Sources: [MN DNR survey](https://files.dnr.state.mn.us/natural_resources/water/lakes/aquatic_plant_reports/31033900_0790.pdf), [MSU Extension](https://www.canr.msu.edu/news/inland_lake_habitats_critical_to_maintaining_healthy_lake_ecosystems)

### Mosses and lichens
- Bright orange sunburst lichen (`#E07A1F`) on shore rocks, grey-green
  rosette lichens (`#A8B096`), black rock tripe; pale reindeer lichen
  cushions (`#C9CDB8`); feather mosses (`#6E8A3A`) on the forest floor,
  sphagnum in bogs. Source: [field notes](https://ninamunteanu.me/tag/lakes/)

## 2. Rocks and terrain

- **Bedrock** is the Canadian Shield: **pink granite** worn smooth and round
  (`#C99A8A`, light `#E0B8A8`, black flecks `#3A3030`), grey gneiss
  (`#8A8A88`), and dark basalt (`#3A3A3C`) weathering rusty (`#6A4A38`).
- **Glacial erratics:** lone rounded boulders on the shore or in the
  shallows, often a different rock from the bedrock; lichen above the
  waterline, dark algae below.
- **Ice-push ridges:** spring ice shoves cobbles into a low berm along the
  waterline.
- **Beaches** are mostly rounded cobble and gravel in grey, pink and black;
  tan sand (`#C8B48A`) in sheltered bays.
- **The lake bottom:** sand, gravel, cobble and boulders in the wave-washed
  shallows (0–6 ft); sand to silt with weed beds (6–20 ft); a fairly steep
  drop-off; then soft brown-black muck (`#2E2A1E`) in the deep. Sunken logs
  from the logging era.
- **Hills, not mountains.** Minnesota's highest point is 2,301 ft. The North
  Shore's "Sawtooth Mountains" are rounded, forested ridges a few hundred
  metres high: no bare peaks, no snow. In the distance they read as layered
  blue-green bands (near `#4A6A6A`, middle `#7A9AA8`, far `#A8BCC8`).
- Sources: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_erratic), [MNFI](https://mnfi.anr.msu.edu/abstracts/ecology/Volcanic_bedrock_lakeshore.pdf), [NAGT](https://cdn.serc.carleton.edu/files/nagt/sections/eastern/fall_2025_nagt-es_bulletin_cb.pdf), [UMN](https://kirkby.esci.umn.edu/displays/rock-garden/glacial-erratic)

## 3. Water

- **Clarity:** Minnesota lakes average 2.25 m (7.4 ft) of visibility; northern
  forest lakes about 3.2 m (10.5 ft); clear lakes 16 ft or more. In the
  north, tannin limits clarity more than algae.
- **Two kinds of lake:**
  - **Clear:** green-teal at the surface, dark teal-blue deep down
    (`#5E8F8A` shallow, `#2F5E66` mid, `#163640` deep, `#0A1A20` bottom).
  - **Tannin-stained ("root beer"), very common:** amber-gold looking down in
    the shallows (`#B88A3A`), brown (`#6A4420`) then near-black olive
    (`#2A2010`) within a few feet; sand looks golden-orange; from above the
    surface is dark and mirror-like. Rivers and waterfalls run root-beer brown
    with **cream-tan foam** (`#E8D8B0`). These lakes are getting browner.
- **Depth takes the colour out:** red goes first, then orange and yellow.
  Things deep down should tint toward the water's colour, not just darken.
- **Light underwater:** sun shafts slant in from the sun's direction and fade
  with depth; caustics (bright wavy nets on the bottom) only in water less
  than about 10 ft deep on sunny days; pale floating motes.
- **The surface:** mirror-calm at dawn and dusk, often with mist on cool
  mornings; wind breaks reflections into horizontal streaks, with calm
  slicks between rippled patches.
- **Through the day:** pink and peach with mist at dawn; sky-blue (`#6A9AC0`)
  where the surface reflects the sky at midday and dark green-brown under the
  shoreline's reflection; orange and magenta at dusk; near-black at night
  (`#0A0F18`) with a column of moon glitter.
- Sources: [UMN Landsat census](https://experts.umn.edu/en/publications/a-20-year-landsat-water-clarity-census-of-minnesotas-10000-lakes/), [UMN Conservancy](https://conservancy.umn.edu/items/96ad4d2f-5934-41fa-ba45-ceb94e0e554f), [UW Limnology](https://blog.limnology.wisc.edu/2020/04/17/water-we-talking-about-why-water-can-look-like-root-beer/), [Eos](https://eos.org/editor-highlights/nutrients-may-change-flavor-of-meadow-tea-in-lakes), [Stanford](https://graphics.stanford.edu/wikis/cs348b-06/alexli/final)

## 4. Sky and light

- **Day length:** in June the sun sets about 9:00–9:15 pm and rises about
  5:15 am; in December it sets about 4:20–4:30 pm (general knowledge).
  Summer evenings stay light remarkably late, and twilight is long: golden
  hour over an hour, blue hour 30–40 minutes, a faint glow in the north in June.
- **Sky gradients** (top → middle → horizon):

  | Time | Top | Middle | Horizon |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | Midday | `#3E7CC4` | `#6FA4D8` | `#C6DDEE` |
  | Golden hour | `#5A86B8` | `#E8B070` | `#F4C878` |
  | Sunset | `#4A4F80` | `#D86A5A` | `#F49A48` |
  | Blue hour | `#1E2A55` | `#3A4A7A` | `#8A6A80` |
  | Night | `#05070F` | `#0B1020` | `#141A2A` |
  | Dawn | `#3A4A78` | `#C88AA0` | `#F2C29A` |

- **Silhouettes:** at golden hour the conifers go near-black blue-green
  (`#141E1C`). The jagged line of fir and spruce spires, with tall white
  pines above them, is *the* north-woods horizon.
- **Clouds:** flat-bottomed fair-weather cumulus building from late morning;
  towering thunderheads on hot afternoons; wispy cirrus and mackerel skies
  ahead of fronts; morning mist; in some summers, wildfire-smoke haze (orange
  sun, milky sky).
- **Aurora:** low green arcs and curtains in the north (`#4BE08A`), red or
  magenta tops in strong storms (`#C04A7A`); a few to a dozen-plus good
  displays a year, most around the equinoxes.
- **Stars:** dark sites show the Milky Way clearly in summer; moon glitter on
  the lake.
- Sources: [sunrisesunset.io](https://sunrisesunset.io/golden-hour-blue-hour/), [PhotographyTalk](https://www.photographytalk.com/golden-hour-vs-blue-hour-when-to-shoot-what-in-landscape-photography/), [Rainy Lake](https://www.rainylake.org/minnesota-vacations/seeing-the-northern-lights-on-rainy-lake), [CBS Minnesota](https://www.cbsnews.com/minnesota/news/northern-lights-alert-the-aurora-borealis-may-be-visible-in-minnesota-monday-tuesday/)

## 5. Docks, boathouses, boats, cabins

- **Docks** are mostly sectional and taken out for winter: 4–8 ft wide
  sections on galvanized steel pipe legs about 8–10 ft apart, decked in cedar
  or treated pine. Older docks sit on rock-filled timber cribs or log posts.
- **Weathering:** sun and rain turn wood **silver-grey** within a season or
  two (`#9A968E` top, `#6E6A64` shade, `#4A4640` gaps); board ends and the
  wet zone at the waterline go green-black with algae (`#3A4030`).
- **A fishing hut on stilts isn't typical here** (more Gulf Coast). The local
  equivalents are a gable-roofed **boathouse** at the waterline, sometimes
  over the water on cribs; a **sauna** by the water; and in winter, **ice
  fishing houses** on the ice. A weathered boathouse on a crib at the dock's
  end would fit.
- **Boats:** riveted aluminium fishing boats (Lund, Crestliner) with a small
  outboard, bare aluminium (`#B8BCC0`) or dark green, blue or maroon;
  aluminium or cedar-strip canoes, dark green (`#2E4A34`) or red; older
  wooden rowboats.
- **Cabins:** dark stained logs (`#5A3E28`) or siding painted barn red
  (`#8E2F25`) or forest green (`#2F4A35`) with white trim; green or black
  roofs; a fieldstone chimney (general knowledge).
- Sources: [Tommy Docks](https://tommydocks.com/products/4f-6ft-8ft-superior-pipe-heavy-duty-shippable-sizes), [HomeAdditionPlus](https://homeadditionplus.com/dev/decks/building-a-pipe-dock), [Wood Doctor](https://www.woodworkingnetwork.com/knowledge-center/wood-dr/what-causes-wood-turn-gray-outdoors), [McIlvain](https://blog.mcilvain.com/wood-siding-silver-gray-patina-species.html), [Hammerton](https://hammerton.com/articles/a-beautiful-adirondack-style-boathouse/), [Forum News](https://www.dglobe.com/opinion/columns/mcfeely-fishing-dreams-begin-in-new-york-mills-as-a-roll-of-aluminum), [MN statutes](https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/2024/cite/97C.355/subd/97C.355.2)

## 6. Wildlife reminders

- **Deer:** red-tan in summer (`#B0703A`), grey-brown in winter (`#7A6A58`);
  white throat patch and belly; the tail's white underside flags when it
  runs; fawns spotted until about September.
- **Red fox:** rusty orange (`#C8602A`), black stockings and ear backs, white
  tail tip.
- **Snowshoe hare:** brown in summer (`#7A5E40`), **white in winter**, dark ear
  tips all year.
- **Black bear:** glossy black, brown muzzle, sometimes a white chest blaze;
  upright ears, straight face.
- **Ring-billed gull:** white, pale grey back, black wingtips with white
  spots, **yellow legs**, yellow bill with a black ring; young are mottled
  brown.
- **Song sparrow:** streaky brown with a **dark spot in the middle of the
  breast**, striped grey-brown face; pumps its tail in flight.
- **Mallard:** as drawn; drakes look like hens ("eclipse") July–September.
- **Bald eagle:** dark brown (`#3A2A1E`), white head and tail; young are
  mottled; perches on the tallest white pine or a dead snag over the water.
- **Common loon** (Minnesota's state bird; the sight and sound of these
  lakes): black head with a green sheen, **red eye**, dagger bill, striped
  white necklace, black back with a **white checkerboard**, white belly; sits
  low and dives silently.
- **Others:** great blue heron in the shallows, belted kingfisher diving from
  the dock, osprey hovering, common mergansers, painted turtles on logs, a
  beaver lodge and V-shaped wake, red squirrels and Canada jays, dragonflies
  and mayfly hatches at dusk.
- Sources: [Loon Preservation Committee](https://loon.org/about-the-common-loon/appearance/), [NY DEC](https://dec.ny.gov/nature/animals-fish-plants/common-loon), [All About Birds](https://www.allaboutbirds.org/guide/Ring-billed_Gull/id), [Audubon](https://www.audubon.org/news/birdist-rule-20-identify-your-first-gull)

## What our scene gets wrong or could add

1. Replace the snowy peaks with low, rounded, forested ridges in two or three
   bands of blue haze.
2. Trees: tiered, flat-topped white pines above the canopy, fir spires, ragged
   black spruce at boggy edges, orange-trunked red pines, white birch clumps,
   pale aspen, cedars leaning over the water.
3. Water: green-teal (clear lake) or tea-brown (tannin lake), darkening fast
   with depth, reds draining out of things as they go deeper.
4. Lakebed: sand, cobble and boulders near shore, then weed beds, then a
   drop-off to dark muck; sunken logs; weeds stop at 15–20 ft.
5. Rocks: pink granite, black basalt, rounded erratics, a cobble berm; orange
   and grey-green lichen above the water, dark algae below.
6. The stilt hut isn't local: consider a weathered boathouse on a crib, or keep
   the hut but gable-roofed and silver-grey with barn-red or green trim; dock
   on pipe legs 8–10 ft apart, silver-grey deck, algae-dark legs at the water.
7. Shore plants: cattails in a mucky bay, bulrush and wild rice in the
   shallows, white lilies and yellow spatterdock, violet pickerelweed.
8. The waterfall: root-beer water over black rock with cream foam.
9. Time of day: late sunsets and long twilight, morning mist and a mirror
   lake, afternoon cumulus and thunderheads, lilies closing in the
   afternoon, moon glitter, now and then a green aurora low in the north.
10. Seasons: gold aspen and birch against dark conifers in fall, tamarack in
    late October, the hare turning white, eclipse drakes, fawns' spots.
11. Cheap animation: fluttering aspen, swaying wild celery, a loon diving far
    off.
12. Add the common loon.
